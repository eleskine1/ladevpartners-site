§ 01 · BRIEF
The units the lot already held.

An ownership group holds a multifamily property in Santa Monica. Under SB 1211, the lot can carry up to eight new detached accessory dwelling units. The question was never whether the units were allowed. It was whether they could be permitted, powered, protected, and priced without the project dying in the gaps between consultants.

We were engaged to hold those gaps. The practice manages the architect, the structural engineer, the electrical engineer, and the permit coordinator as one team, against one schedule, with one fee model reconciled line by line against the city's adopted fee schedule. Deposits are paid. The set is moving through plan check.

No single decision on this project is exotic. What makes it work is that every decision was made early, in writing, and in the right order.

§ 02 · METHOD
The quiet items are the critical path.

On a project like this, the drawings are rarely what kills the schedule. The utilities are.

Eight new units on an occupied lot means new electrical service, and new service means the utility's timeline, not the city's. We engaged the utility's pre-planning process early, sized the new service around two meter packs, and treated the service request as a critical-path item from the first month rather than a discovery at rough-in.

The same discipline applies to fire and life safety. The separation between structures decides whether sprinklers are triggered, so the site plan was drawn around that threshold deliberately, not adjusted after a correction notice. Occupancy classification was reconciled across the set before submittal, because an inconsistency between sheets is the kind of small thing that costs a full recheck cycle.

And the fees: we built a workbook reconciling every anticipated fee against the city's adopted schedule, so the ownership group has seen the whole cost of entitlement in one place, before committing to construction.

Nothing on this project is clever. Everything on it is early. That is the whole method, and it is harder than it sounds.
